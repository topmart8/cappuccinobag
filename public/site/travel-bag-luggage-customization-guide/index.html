Travel Product Specification

Travel Bag & Luggage Customization Guide

Start with the trip, packing list and transport transitions. Those decisions determine the useful volume, opening, organization and carry system more reliably than a generic “weekender” or “luggage” label.

Review My Travel Bag BriefExplore Travel Bag Development

Describe one complete travel scenario

State the typical trip length, transport modes, target user and whether the bag is carried alone or with other luggage. Explain where it must fit—such as a vehicle space, hotel room, locker or buyer-defined transport limit—without assuming one universal cabin standard. Destination and carrier requirements should be checked by the buyer for the intended market.

Convert the packing list into dimensions

Group clothing, footwear, devices, toiletries, documents and small essentials, then estimate their packed orientation. Confirm whether the concept depends on folding, compression or a rigid item such as a laptop. External dimensions, usable internal space and pocket volume are different measures, so approve the physical sample with representative contents.

Choose an opening for the packing behaviour

A clamshell opening supports organized packing, a wide top opening suits quick loading and a narrower zipper can reduce panel interruption. Mark where the opening stops, how it behaves when the bag is full and whether the user needs access while the bag remains upright. Check that internal dividers do not block the hinge or zipper path.

Prioritize travel organization

Separate only the items that create a clear handling need: shoes, laundry, toiletries, documents, electronics or a water bottle. Decide whether each zone must be removable, ventilated, wipe-clean or simply easy to reach. Too many fixed pockets can reduce usable space and make the bag less adaptable across different trips.

Plan every carry transition

Review how the bag moves from home to vehicle, station or airport, through walking sections and into storage. Specify handle positions, shoulder or backpack straps, strap storage and any luggage-handle attachment. Sampling should check balance when fully packed, clearance around hardware and whether unused straps interfere with wheels, openings or the user.

Set protection and material priorities

Identify base contact, corner wear, hardware exposure and the items that need padding. Choose shell, lining, reinforcement, zipper and hardware requirements around weight, structure, abrasion, cleaning and appearance. Water-resistance or protection wording must reflect the full agreed construction and any project-specific verification, not only the fabric name.

Run a packing-led sample check

Load the sample with the planned contents and inspect finished measurements, zipper access, divider positions, device fit, shoe or laundry zones, handle balance, strap adjustment and storage compatibility. Confirm logo orientation in each carry mode. Record approved changes and the final packing method before the product becomes a production reference.

Prepare the travel-bag RFQ

Send the travel scenario, packing list, desired format, target dimensions, opening, organization, carry modes, material priorities, branding, packaging, destination market and estimated quantity. Annotated references should explain what to copy, change or avoid. This guide supports specification planning while the travel product hub remains the primary collection and sourcing destination.
