Brand Application Planning

Bag Logo Customization Guide

The right logo method follows the face material, artwork detail, placement, expected handling and desired brand effect—not the method name alone.

Request an Artwork ReviewView OEM/ODM Development

Match the logo method to the surface

Begin with the exact material and panel construction. Embroidery needs enough space and support for stitching; printing depends on surface texture, colour and the selected ink or transfer process; debossing and embossing require a material that can hold the impression; metal badges and patches need attachment points and backing. A method that works on a flat swatch may need adjustment near padding, seams, curves or pockets.

Prepare production artwork, not a screenshot

Supply editable vector artwork where possible, together with approved colours, minimum clear space and any rule for single-colour use. Identify which fine lines, small text or gradients are essential. If the source artwork cannot be reproduced at the requested size, approve a simplified production version rather than leaving the factory to interpret the brand mark.

Fix position and scale on the product

Mark the logo location on a dimensioned drawing or product image and state the finished width or height. Review how the mark relates to seams, zipper paths, pocket openings, handle attachments and the way the bag sits when carried. For a coordinated range, define whether every product uses one physical logo size or a proportional size system.

Compare visual effect with construction impact

A flat print can preserve detailed artwork, embroidery adds thread and texture, an embossed or debossed mark creates a quieter tonal effect, and a badge becomes a separate component. Each choice can affect cost, weight, panel backing and sample preparation differently. Select the effect that supports the product position and construction instead of combining several techniques without a clear brand hierarchy.

Approve a representative logo sample

Review colour, dimensions, edge quality, stitch density or impression depth, alignment and attachment on the intended material. Confirm the logo under the same panel conditions as the product where practical. For multiple colours or materials, identify which combinations need separate approval because contrast and surface behaviour may change.

What to send for a logo quotation

Provide the product reference, face material, vector logo, preferred method, finished size, position, colour reference, number of logo variants, packaging presentation and estimated quantity. Add any restrictions on backing, visible stitching or metal finish. These inputs let the project team review feasibility and quotation factors while the specific embossing, debossing or metal-logo construction is confirmed within the individual project.
