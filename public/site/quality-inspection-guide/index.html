Buyer Quality Planning

Quality Inspection Guide

Build a practical inspection plan for custom bags and accessories by linking approved specifications, samples, workmanship points and packaging requirements.

Discuss a ProjectView Development Process

Define the inspection reference

An inspection needs an agreed product specification, approved sample, artwork and packaging instructions. Record dimensions, tolerances, materials, colours, hardware, logo placement and functional details before production. Without a shared reference, buyer and factory may judge the same variation differently.

Check incoming components against approvals

Compare shell, lining, webbing, zipper, hardware and decoration components with the approved direction before assembly. Confirm colour and appearance under consistent conditions and verify that visible components match the bill of materials. Any substitute should be reviewed rather than assumed acceptable.

Inspect construction and workmanship

Focus on seam alignment, stitch condition, edge finishing, zipper installation, hardware operation, reinforcement and cleanliness. Product-specific checks may include pocket access, divider position, strap adjustment or assembled shape. Inspection criteria should reflect the actual specification instead of a generic checklist alone.

Verify dimensions, fit and function

Measure the agreed control points and test the bag with representative contents. Open and close zippers, adjust straps, operate buckles and confirm that compartments fit the intended equipment. Where a project defines a particular performance requirement, use the method and acceptance criteria agreed for that project.

Review branding and packaging

Check logo method, size, position, orientation and colour against approved artwork. Confirm labels, inserts, protective packing, carton marks and unit counts before shipment. Packaging review matters because an otherwise acceptable product can still arrive with incorrect identification or presentation.

Close issues with traceable decisions

Record findings with clear photos, quantities and locations. Separate critical functional issues from appearance variations and confirm the disposition of affected units. Buyers should retain the final specification, approval history and inspection record so repeat orders start from the same agreed standard.

Padel-specific inspection

What Should Buyers Check on a Padel Bag?

Inspect a padel bag against the approved sample and project specification. Use the intended rackets, shoes and representative packed load so fit and carry decisions reflect real use.

Racket fit and compartment protection

Load the intended racket size and count. Check head and handle clearance, zipper movement, opening access, padding position and contact points so the compartment closes without pressing or rubbing the equipment.

Shoe-zone fit and ventilation

Where a shoe zone is specified, confirm usable fit, opening access, lining coverage and the approved ventilation construction. Check whether footwear interferes with racket, clothing or accessory space when the bag is packed.

Loaded carry and stress areas

Pack the agreed load and review balance in every intended carry mode. Inspect shoulder-strap anchors, handles, base corners, seam ends and other load-bearing areas for construction that matches the approved sample.

Zippers, seams, lining and pocket access

Operate every zipper through its full path and check that lining or seam allowance does not catch. Review stitching at curved openings, dividers and reinforced zones, then confirm each compartment remains usable with the bag loaded.

Branding position and appearance

Compare logo size, position, orientation, colour and finish with approved artwork in the normal packed and carried shape. Also confirm labels, zipper pullers and lining details where they form part of the approved brand specification.

Packaging and shape protection

Confirm inserts, stuffing, protective covers, unit packaging and carton arrangement against the packing instruction. The packed product should retain the approved shape and avoid pressure on structured panels, racket zones or branded surfaces.
